ABSTRACT 
 
The Aadhaar ecosystem plays a critical role in identity verification across banking, government ser-
vices, and digital platforms. However, increasing cases of identity theft, document tampering, and forged 
Aadhaar cards highlight major security vulnerabilities in existing manual and semi-automated verification 
processes. These traditional methods are slow, error-prone, and incapable of detecting sophisticated fraud 
such as manipulated photos, altered text, or fake biometric data. To address these challenges, this project 
proposes an AI-Powered Aadhaar Fraud Management System designed to deliver fast, accurate, and multi-
layered verification using advanced machine learning models. 
The system integrates three core AI components to ensure comprehensive fraud detection. A Convo-
lutional Neural Network (CNN) model analyzes Aadhaar card images to detect document forgery, pixel ma-
nipulation, and digital tampering. An Optical Character Recognition (OCR) engine, powered by Tesser-
act/PaddleOCR, automatically extracts textual information such as Aadhaar number, name, and date of birth 
for validation. In parallel, a Facial Recognition module cross-verifies the photograph on the Aadhaar card 
with a live-captured user image, supported by liveness detection to prevent spoofing, impersonation, and use 
of printed or digital photos. 
To further strengthen security, the system performs cross-verification of OCR-extracted data with the 
encrypted information embedded in the Aadhaar QR code. A Fraud Scoring Engine aggregates outputs from 
all AI modules and generates a risk score, enabling instant decisions and automated alerts for high-risk cases. 
The entire workflow is deployed using FastAPI for backend processing, ensuring scalability, low-latency 
model execution, and secure communication with the frontend. Cloud storage or databases such as Post-
greSQL/MongoDB manage verification logs, audit trails, and model outputs. 
This integrated approach enables real-time, reliable, and intelligent Aadhaar verification, significantly 
reducing the possibility of fraud and enhancing trust in digital identity systems. By replacing manual valida-
tion with automated AI analysis, the system improves accuracy, reduces processing time, strengthens privacy 
protection, and provides a future-ready solution for banking, government services, and organizations relying 
on Aadhaar-based authentication.

CHAPTER 2 
 LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 Literature Review 
Aadhaar, India’s largest digital identity system, plays a critical role in authentication for banking, telecom, 
welfare schemes, and digital services. However, the growing dependency on Aadhaar-based verification 
has also increased opportunities for fraud, including forged Aadhaar cards, tampered demographic details, 
manipulated images, and impersonation attacks. Several studies and existing systems attempt to strengthen 
identity verification, yet significant gaps remain—particularly in the area of automated, AI-driven fraud de-
tection. 
Traditional Aadhaar verification relies on manual inspection or basic digital tools that only check surface-
level features such as text clarity or QR code readability. These approaches fail to detect advanced digital 
forgeries, synthetic identities, or deepfake-based impersonation. Research in document forensics indicates 
that Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can effectively detect pixel-level forgery, splicing, and tam-
pering, but real-world Aadhaar verification systems rarely adopt such techniques. Likewise, OCR-based 
systems efficiently extract text from documents, but without cross-verification against secure data sources 
(e.g., encrypted QR code), OCR outputs alone are insufficient for reliable authentication. 
Studies in biometric verification highlight that facial recognition combined with liveness detection signifi-
cantly reduces impersonation attempts. However, most existing Aadhaar verification processes rely only on 
comparing printed photographs, making them vulnerable to photo swaps and morphed images. Addition-
ally, emerging research emphasizes multi-model fusion—combining image analysis, OCR, facial recogni-
tion, and metadata validation—to improve detection accuracy, yet commercial solutions often lack such 
comprehensive integration. 
Government initiatives like Aadhaar QR code verification provide validated demographic details, but they 
do not detect forged images, manipulated backgrounds, or facial mismatches. Meanwhile, financial institu-
tions and service providers face challenges as they depend on outdated or manual checks due to the absence 
of intelligent, automated systems. 
This gap highlights the need for a holistic AI-powered verification solution that can simultaneously ana-
lyze visual, textual, and biometric elements of an Aadhaar card. The proposed system addresses this critical 
requirement by integrating CNN-based forgery detection, OCR-based text extraction, QR data validation, 
and facial recognition with liveness checks—thereby offering a real-time, end-to-end fraud detection plat-
form. 
2.2 Key Takeaways from Literature Review 
 Existing Aadhaar verification methods are manual, slow, and highly prone to error. 
 Traditional tools do not detect digital forgery, image manipulation, or biometric mismatches. 
 AI techniques like CNNs, OCR, and facial recognition can significantly improve fraud detection 
accuracy. 
 Current systems lack multi-model AI integration, making them incapable of identifying complex 
fraud attempts.

CHAPTER 4 
SYSTEM DESIGN AND ARCHITECTURE 
4.1 System Overview 
The AI-Powered Aadhaar Fraud Management System is developed to provide a fully automated, highly 
accurate, and real-time identity verification mechanism. The system integrates multiple artificial intelli-
gence models—each performing a different stage of verification—to ensure that Aadhaar cards submitted 
for authentication are genuine and free from any form of tampering. Modern Aadhaar fraud involves so-
phisticated manipulation of images, demographic text, and photographs; therefore, a unified multi-model 
verification approach is necessary to detect complex forgery attempts. 
The system operates by taking one or two images of the Aadhaar card (front side mandatory, back side op-
tional), processing the images using a structured verification pipeline, and outputting a fraud score repre-
senting the likelihood of manipulation. The verification pipeline consists of several independent but inter-
connected modules: 
 Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for text detection 
 CNN-based forgery detection for identifying manipulated regions 
 QR code extraction and secure decoding 
 Facial recognition and liveness detection to prevent impersonation 
 Fraud scoring engine to combine outputs into a final decision 
Each module is independently testable and has its own workflow. Together, they form a composite verifica-
tion engine that can detect a wide range of fraud scenarios including forged photos, altered demographic 
fields, mismatched QR data, and deepfake-based impersonation attempts. 
The system has been developed using a modular architecture that allows each component to operate inde-
pendently while contributing to a centralized decision-making layer. This modularity increases scalability, 
simplifies debugging, and allows individual components to be upgraded with minimal impact to the rest of 
the system. FastAPI serves as the backend engine responsible for coordinating module execution, data ex-
change, and response generation. 
The overall architecture has been designed to support real-time verification with minimal latency, even 
when multiple AI models run during the analysis. The integration strategy ensures that the system is both 
efficient and resilient in practical environments such as banking, telecom verification centers, digital 
onboarding systems, and government service portals. 
4.2 System Architecture 
The architecture of the Aadhaar Fraud Detection System is logically divided into five major layers, each 
responsible for a specific set of functionalities. The layered design promotes clean separation of concerns, 
scalability, and ease of maintenance. 
 
 
4.2.1 Presentation Layer (Frontend User Interface) 
The presentation layer provides the interface through which the user interacts with the system. This layer is 
built using React, HTML, and CSS, offering a simple and minimalistic interface optimized for quick doc-
ument processing and easy interpretation of results.  
                                                                                     11                                              
 
Key responsibilities of this layer include: 
 Capturing Aadhaar card images through file inputs 
 Allowing optional QR verification via a checkbox 
 Sending the inputs to the backend through HTTP POST requests 
 Displaying verification results such as fraud score, detected fields, and mismatch indicators 
 Presenting errors clearly if the upload or backend process fails 
The interface is intentionally simple because the system’s goal is functional verification rather than user 
interaction complexity. This design also supports future extensions such as role-based dashboards (admin, 
verifier, auditor). 
 
4.2.2 Application Layer (FastAPI Backend Controller) 
The application layer functions as the “brain” of the system, handling the coordination between user inputs 
and AI modules. Built using FastAPI, it ensures high performance, asynchronous request handling, and 
efficient coordination of heavy AI tasks. 
Responsibilities of this layer include: 
 Receiving files from the frontend 
 Invoking AI modules in a sequential or parallel order 
 Preprocessing images before sending them to models 
 Managing exceptions and validation errors 
 Aggregating results from OCR, CNN, QR, and face recognition modules 
 Sending the final fraud score and structured results back to the UI 
 Logging key events for audit and debugging 
FastAPI is particularly suited for this system because of its speed, async support, and ease of integration 
with Python’s AI ecosystem (TensorFlow, PyTorch, OpenCV, Tesseract). 
 
4.2.3 AI Processing Layer (Core Fraud Detection Models) 
This is the most critical layer of the system. It contains and executes the various AI models responsible for 
Aadhaar verification. 
The AI Processing Layer consists of the following components: 
1. CNN-Based Forgery Detection Model 
This model analyzes the Aadhaar image to detect signs of tampering using image segmentation, texture 
analysis, and feature comparison. CNNs are effective in identifying: 
 Manipulated photographs 
 Altered digits in Aadhaar number 
 Modified text fields 
 Splicing or copy-move attacks 
 Replaced backgrounds and shadows 
The model outputs: 
 Probability score (0 to 1) indicating the likelihood of forgery  
                                                                                     12                                              
 
 Heatmap of tampered regions (backend use) 
2. OCR Extraction Engine 
OCR is used to extract demographic fields from the Aadhaar card image. Tesseract or PaddleOCR reads 
textual content such as: 
 Name 
 Date of Birth 
 Gender 
 Aadhaar number 
 Address (if available) 
Preprocessing (binarization, noise removal, cropping) ensures accuracy even with low-quality images. 
3. QR Code Decoder 
Aadhaar cards contain a secure QR code (encrypted using UIDAI standards). The decoder extracts and de-
crypts QR content to retrieve official demographic data. 
This secure data is cross-verified with OCR results to detect discrepancies. 
4. Facial Recognition and Liveness Detection 
This component extracts the face from the Aadhaar card and compares it with a live-captured image. 
Liveness detection helps prevent: 
 Printed photo spoofing 
 Screen-based spoofing 
 Deepfake attempts 
5. Feature Aggregation Module 
Outputs from all AI models are collected and sent to the Fraud Scoring Engine. 
 
4.2.4 Fraud Scoring & Decision Layer 
This layer consolidates the outputs from different AI modules and generates a unified fraud score based on 
weighted logic. 
Responsibilities include: 
 Combining CNN forgery scores 
 Checking consistency between OCR text and QR code data 
 Calculating face match similarity 
 Detecting missing or inconsistent fields 
 Applying thresholds to classify the result into HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW risk 
The fraud score is a numeric value (0–100) assigned based on the severity of mismatches, inconsistencies, 
and the output of the forgery detection network. 
This layer ensures objective and transparent decision-making by using quantifiable factors, reducing de-
pendence on manual judgment.

4.3 High-Level Design (HLD) 
HLD describes how major modules interact during the verification workflow. 
 
4.3.1 Document Upload & Preprocessing Module 
This module handles input acquisition and prepares the Aadhaar images for downstream analysis. 
Key tasks include: 
 Accepting JPG/PNG/PDF images 
 Resizing them to standard dimensions 
 Converting to grayscale for OCR 
 Deskewing rotated images 
 Performing noise reduction 
 Cropping ROI (Region of Interest) 
Preprocessing ensures consistent input quality, which directly affects OCR and forgery detection accuracy. 
 
4.3.2 OCR Data Extraction Module 
After preprocessing, OCR reads the text regions and extracts demographic data. The module uses EAST or 
CRAFT for text detection and Tesseract/PaddleOCR for text recognition. 
Output fields: 
 Name 
 DOB 
 Gender 
 Aadhaar number 
 Address (if back side uploaded) 
OCR results are passed to the Cross-Verification Module for consistency checking. 
 
4.3.3 QR Code Verification Module 
This module performs encrypted QR extraction using UIDAI’s official guidelines. 
Steps include: 
 Locating QR code in the image 
 Extracting and decoding it 
 Decrypting the embedded data 
 Comparing demographic fields with OCR output 
Any mismatch (Name, DOB, Aadhaar number, Gender) indicates possible fraud. 
 
4.3.4 CNN-Based Forgery Detection Module 
This model processes the Aadhaar image for digital manipulation. 
It checks for: 
 Replaced photos

 Altered text blocks 
 Pasted digits 
 Background anomalies 
 Morphing artifacts 
Output: 
 Forgery score 
 Tampered region map (for backend analysis) 
 
4.3.5 Facial Recognition & Liveness Detection Module 
This module performs identity confirmation by comparing: 
 Photo from Aadhaar card 
 Live user photo 
It generates: 
 Embedding vectors 
 Similarity score 
 Liveness probability (anti-spoofing) 
 
4.3.6 Fraud Scoring Engine 
The fraud scoring system assigns weighted values to each model output. 
Example weight distribution: 
 CNN Forgery Score → 40% 
 QR Consistency → 25% 
 OCR–QR Matching → 15% 
 Face Similarity → 20% 
The final score classifies the Aadhaar card into: 
 HIGH RISK 
 MEDIUM RISK 
 LOW RISK

The LLD provides detailed internal workflows of each module. 
4.4.1 OCR Module (LLD) 
Steps: 
1. Convert Aadhaar image to grayscale. 
2. Apply thresholding or adaptive binarization. 
3. Extract text regions using EAST or CRAFT detector. 
4. Recognize text using OCR. 
5. Parse fields (Name, DOB, Gender, ID). 
6. Extract QR code and decode.

5. Heatmap identifies tampered regions.

 Forgery score 
 Location of tampered regions (if any) 
4.4.3 Facial Recognition & Liveness Module (LLD)

3. Generate embeddings for both images. 
4. Calculate cosine similarity score. 
5. Perform blink detection / texture analysis for liveness. 
6. Return match score & spoof probability.

 Face Match → 20% 
 
Steps:

5. Display decision to user.

CONCLUSION 
The AI-Powered Aadhaar Fraud Management System was developed with the objective of enhancing 
the reliability, accuracy, and security of identity verification processes that depend heavily on Aadhaar au-
thentication. Traditional manual verification methods are prone to human error and increasingly ineffective 
against modern threats such as digital forgery, sophisticated image manipulation, and impersonation attacks. 
By integrating AI techniques—CNN-based forgery detection, OCR-driven text extraction, QR code valida-
